Wheel Speed Simulator

Compare an active Hall-effect sensor with a passive variable-reluctance sensor.

To catch a fault that repeats once per revolution, try 20 km/h with the 500 ms scope window, then freeze the trace.

Active Hall-effect sensor

The powered sensor switches between two signal levels as the magnetic poles pass. Increasing wheel speed raises the frequency while the signal levels stay the same.

Passive variable-reluctance sensor

Steel teeth and gaps change the magnetic flux through the pickup coil. Faster rotation increases both the frequency and the induced AC voltage. At rest the output is 0 V.

Teaching model with a 2.0 m tire circumference, 24 magnetic pole pairs and 24 steel teeth. The Hall trace represents a 0–5 V switched output. Passive amplitude and fault severity are illustrative. The corrosion example uses a 1 kΩ module load and a 2.5 V switching threshold.
