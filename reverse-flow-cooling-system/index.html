Improvised head-first reverse-flow teaching diagram: coolant enters the heads, passes downward through the block and returns through rear pump passages to the hot-side chamber. The thermostat directs return coolant to either the internal bypass or the upper radiator hose; the lower radiator hose supplies the inlet-side poppet. The complete upper cylinder-head gallery and its vertical head jets use one warm colour representing rapid heat absorption; the external pump supply line retains its inlet temperature. The heater feed shares that same warm head colour. Head metal warms faster than block metal; descending block-jacket traces follow the heated head outlet and the slower-warming block; the dashboard displays the modeled ECT reading. The bottom degas supply joins the pump suction circuit through a Y-fitting, while small upper lines serve as air bleeds. The bottle warms slowly during early warm-up and heats rapidly toward engine-coolant temperature as the thermostat opens; its liquid colour is modeled separately from engine coolant. At idle in automatic mode the low fan cycles on at 93°C and off at 91°C. The red coolant symbol illuminates only after the displayed temperature exceeds three-quarters of the gauge sweep. The amber check-engine symbol illuminates after a sustained mismatch between modeled engine metal temperature and ECT reading. Low coolant still commands HIGH fan speed as an illustrative fail-safe; heater-core heat can be weak at idle and drop off with higher RPM as air disrupts circulation. Internal pump passages are simplified for instruction, not an exact factory casting.
